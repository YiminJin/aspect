## 3. Implement prediction and recovery through the timestep manager

Add a fault-aware ASPECT mesh refinement criterion and use existing timestep reactions for scheduling. The current branch has `refine_and_advance` and `refine_and_repeat_step`. Inspect the manager's reaction priority and retry-duration reduction. The main loop decides acceptance before publishing material history, and invokes mesh refinement before repeating or advancing. Work with that ordering. Keep core changes small and general whenever an existing hook cannot express a required operation.

1. **Predict from accepted geometry.** Keep the formed fault band fine. Restore a disk of radius 4ℓ around the accepted forward tip whenever its distance to the nearest under-resolved cell falls below 3ℓ. Measure distance to cell footprints inside the domain. The physical boundary is not an under-resolved cell. The accepted 2ℓ direction neighborhood should have room to turn. Check cheaply at each accepted interval and remesh only when coverage needs extending. Mark to the required level before spending another expensive nonlinear solve, even if multiple mesh-only refinement passes are needed.
2. **Test the energy warning.** In a flat intact AT1 neighborhood, use the shear-driving term already consumed by phase assembly relative to Kf = Gc/[(8/3)ℓ]. In the diffuse band, use the one-sided unprojected phase residual for an admissible positive perturbation, including the gradient and face terms with consistent normalization. Evaluate a forecast from information available at decision time and keep its direction and threshold separate from the physical growth law. Compare warning fractions 0.7 and 0.9 offline on advancing frames, if available. If the granite tip is quiet, use a verified small growing case. Implement and exercise the disk predictor in production. Add the energy warning to runtime marking if it anticipates missed growth or reduces total work in a discriminating comparison. Otherwise report the comparison and retain the simpler runtime rule. Do not use RSF slip speed as tip speed.
3. **Handle a missed prediction.** Before committing a converged trial, detect new seed-connected damage in an under-resolved region with a documented field/increment tolerance, tested against physical contours rather than floating-point positivity. Distinguish a true miss during the interval from a valid endpoint that merely needs more room for the *next* interval. For a miss, carry the requested physical region through rollback, discard the trial, refine the accepted parent, then repeat the same physical interval. An independently required RSF or nonlinear cutback may shorten it. Do not publish trial sections, clocks, diagnostics or work as accepted. Ensure every retry improves the mesh or reports exactly why the requested resolution is unreachable.
4. **Exercise all three decisions.** Demonstrate ordinary acceptance on an unchanged mesh, acceptance followed by preventive refinement, and rejection followed by refinement. Include one simultaneous mesh and timestep rejection. Check exactly one accepted state, age increment and work entry. Compare keyed physical fields on 1 and 2 ranks and after restart.
