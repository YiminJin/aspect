PURPOSE AND AUTHORIZATION

Carry out an architectural refactor of the phase-field fault extension. The goal
is code that an ASPECT developer can understand, use, and modify through familiar
module responsibilities and interfaces. The user should be able to connect the
physical equations to the implementation and identify where to change a material
law, history discretization, geometry operation, or coupling algorithm.

Remove source/simulator/solver/mature_material_step.cc, its special timestep-driver
entry points, and all machinery whose purpose is to support that driver. Do not
recreate the same architecture under another filename, class, callback, or
"generic" step executor. The ordinary simulator timestep and solver-scheme flow
must remain the place where the simulation advances.

algorithm through a dedicated single-step driver. It authorizes implementation,
including the interface and ownership changes needed to complete the refactor.
Exercise judgment across the affected extension; do not limit the work to a
literal file deletion or stop after producing an inventory or proposal. Make a
brief architectural plan, then implement and verify it. Routine design decisions
within this scope do not require another permission request.

ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENTS

1. Remove the dedicated driver and its supporting architecture completely.

Trace its declarations, dispatch conditions, wrapper functions, callback plumbing,
helper types, build entries, and tests. Remove the pieces that exist solely to
support this special driver. Update current documentation and examples that make
it the algorithm's entry point. Preserve historical reports as historical evidence.

The necessary stress, geometry, projection, and acceptance operations still have
to occur. Place them under the appropriate existing modules and their normal
lifecycle. Review the recent helper headers and extracted functions by actual
responsibility: useful computations and explicit dependencies may be incorporated
into those modules, but driver-specific scaffolding must disappear. A blanket
reset that also loses the direct-stress implementation or numerical fixes is not
the requested solution.

2. Establish real responsibility and ownership boundaries.

Use the following responsibilities to guide the design; they do not prescribe a
new class hierarchy or one source file per row:

- Simulator and solver schemes: overall timestep ordering, solver dispatch, and
  the decision to accept or reject an attempt.
- Material model and its internal components: constitutive laws, material
  parameters, localization semantics, physical history updates, and responses
  needed by assembly and linearization.
- History discretization: owned coefficients and FE infrastructure, transport,
  projection, transfer, and well-defined accepted/candidate state access. Keep
  numerical operations distinguishable from constitutive laws.
- Reconstructed-fault infrastructure: geometry, associations, interpolation,
  generic surface-property storage and persistence. The geometry manager must
  not interpret a friction law simply because it stores that law's state.
- Coupled solver and assemblers: bulk/surface equations, condensation, tangents,
  nonlinear iteration, and the projection-consistency iteration where required.
- Postprocessors and benchmark support: observations, audits, and reference-only
  experiments, with no hidden ownership of physical advancement.

Keep the actual global coupling in the solver layer. A material evaluation must
not secretly run a global solve, advance geometry, or publish accepted history.
Conversely, generic simulator code should not manipulate T/R/B, localization
formulas, or private constitutive coefficients.

Reuse the existing registration and extension mechanisms where appropriate.
Where a core extension is necessary, make it small and specific to a reusable
capability. Do not invent a plugin framework, universal transaction engine, or
configurable pipeline. Do not route ordering-sensitive operations through an
unrelated signal merely to avoid touching core code. Keep dependencies and the
order of operations visible at their legitimate call sites.

3. Refactor the connected implementation, including the dg_* organization.

Review source/material_model/phase_field_fault/, source/reconstructed_fault/,
and the affected simulator/solver and assembler interfaces together. Follow
responsibilities across directory boundaries. Consolidate fragmented helpers,
reduce unnecessary public methods and dependencies, and use private components
where they provide an actual ownership or algorithm boundary.

Merely moving methods of one oversized class into more files is insufficient.
Equally, collecting everything into a large source file is insufficient. Choose
names and file divisions that expose physical and numerical responsibilities.
DG is a valid discretization name, but should not be the only explanation of
what a component owns. Avoid reorganizing unrelated ASPECT subsystems.

4. Make the direct physical-stress implementation understandable on its own.

In direct mode the physical tensor history is tau. Its transport, rotation,
reaction, and mechanical observation must be traceable without following T/R/B
reconstruction. Distinguish accepted history, transported/rotated state, candidate
stress, and derived caches. Eliminate misleading use of "trial_stress" for
physical tau in the active internal interface; confine unavoidable legacy names
to an explicit compatibility/serialization boundary.

If the separated representation remains needed by existing configurations or
comparisons, isolate its representation-specific calculations. Share genuinely
common spatial operators and the coupled solver. Do not create another complete
timestep implementation for either representation. Current numerical defaults
and supported configurations are to remain unchanged in this structural task;
their preservation does not require mixing their internal state semantics.

The direct-stress guide and example must explicitly select
Material stress representation = direct physical stress. Explain retained
representations in a short separate comparison. Diagnostic Z must remain
diagnostic in direct mode; the physical localized slip-rate source remains part
of the constitutive law.

5. Preserve the validated algorithm while changing its organization.

Preserve transport/geometry/rotation/reaction ordering, accepted-velocity spin
lag, coefficient sampling, projection and quadrature, friction/age conventions,
the converged final slip guess, and nonlinear acceptance criteria. Preserve
single publication of a joint bulk/surface/geometry candidate and restoration
after rejection or failure. Trial evaluations must use the same accepted parent.

Keep checkpoint identity and MPI ownership/collective semantics correct. Do not
add checkpoint conversion. Maintain supported stationary and moving cases and
the distinct cohesive history semantics. Removing the driver must not disable
the moving-fault capability or introduce a second acceptance authority.

This task does not select a new integrator, stress space, limiter, endpoint law,
physical default, or solver tolerance. If a genuine numerical defect is exposed,
identify it separately rather than disguising a behavior change as refactoring.
