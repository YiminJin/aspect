TASK: Make the phase-field fault code readable, documented, and consistent with ASPECT

GOAL

Improve the implementation so that an ASPECT developer can open its headers and
source files, understand the physical quantities and algorithm, and modify it
without reconstructing its meaning from historical reports. Comments, names,
layout, and class responsibilities all contribute to this goal. The user also
authorizes sensible joining/splitting of structures, classes, and files where it
reduces the effort required to understand the code.

Implement and verify the changes. Do not stop at an audit or proposal, and do not
ask for approval of ordinary decisions within this task. This is a continuation
of the architectural work, with readability of the actual source as the primary
acceptance criterion. Retain the ordinary ASPECT timestep flow; do not restore
the rejected special timestep driver or its supporting architecture.

CONFIRMED PROBLEMS TO ADDRESS

- phase_field_fault.h declares several material-history functions with unnamed
  parameters and little or no explanation of their contracts.
- material_history_internal.h has short type comments but leaves many members,
  including time/dt, rollback fields, and correction state, unexplained.
- stress_reaction.cc, stress_evolution.cc, and physical_stress.cc contain dense

1. APPLY THE ACTUAL ASPECT STYLE

Read contrib/utilities/indent and contrib/utilities/astyle.rc. At the reviewed
revision they require AStyle 2.04, GNU style, and two-space indentation. The
wrapper also handles nested C++ template brackets and whitespace cleanup.
Use that workflow on a scoped list of affected files. The full script traverses
the repository and uses ten processes: do not run it indiscriminately or exceed
the four-worker local budget. AStyle with default settings or a newly invented
clang-format configuration is not an equivalent replacement.

Compare the result with established ASPECT headers and implementations, including
material_model/rheology/elasticity.h and its source. Follow their declaration,
namespace, function, argument-continuation, and Doxygen conventions. Verify
formatter idempotence. Manually repair awkward constructs that automatic
formatting leaves difficult to read, then check formatting again.

Expand compressed statements and nested loops. Separate logically distinct
operations with whitespace and concise comments. Give nontrivial function
parameters names in headers as well as definitions. Use meaningful local names
for state and geometry; ordinary mathematical indices and well-defined symbols
remain appropriate. Preserve expression evaluation, lifetime, and locking scope
when expanding statements. A declaration with several unrelated members should
be split so each member can be understood and documented.

2. DOCUMENT CONTRACTS WHERE DEVELOPERS READ THEM

Use proper Doxygen comments on classes/structures, nontrivial functions, and
state members, including private internal APIs where most of this algorithm
currently resides. A reader should learn the following where applicable:
- What physical or numerical quantity is represented, its units and tensor frame.
- Whether it is accepted state, an anchor, a candidate, scratch, or a cache.
- Inputs, output, mutation, and the meaning of each non-obvious parameter.
- Who owns the data and how long borrowed references or returned views remain valid.
- Preconditions and supported dimensions/discretizations/configurations.
- MPI owned/ghost expectations, collective participation, and thread-safety needs.
- What invalidates a cache, whether it is shared across states, and whether it is
  serialized or rebuilt after restart.

Document the meaning of callback arguments and return entries, not just the
std::function type. Distinguish end-of-step time from interval length, accepted
velocity from a Newton iterate, and raw stored tensors from physical stress.
Verify these descriptions against callers and implementation; do not infer them
from a name alone.

In source files, add short introductions to complicated algorithms and comments
at meaningful stages. Explain why an operation is needed and the invariant or
equation it implements. For example, explain the whole-field mass projection,
the fixed rotated anchor used across nonlinear iterations, the projection
correction, and the order of candidate binding and publication/rollback.

Define mathematical quantities close to their use. Explain the relation between
eta_ve and kappa, the factor of two in twice_localized_slip_rate, the plane-strain
zz component, and representation-dependent meanings. Distinguish diagnostic Z
from mechanical history in direct mode. Explain numerical constants such as the
2-D Q2 local size and transport substep controls from their actual role; do not
invent a theoretical justification or imply unsupported generality.

Comments must describe the implemented method accurately. If code and the
current mathematical contract disagree, report the discrepancy; do not hide it
with a reassuring comment or silently change the physics. Avoid comment quotas,
line-by-line paraphrases, stale stage labels, and repeated full derivations.
The external reading guide supplements in-source documentation; it cannot
substitute for it.

3. MAKE TARGETED STRUCTURAL IMPROVEMENTS

The following are reviewer recommendations based on the actual source. Inspect
their callers and implement the ones that establish a clearer boundary. Choose
the concrete names and composition using repository conventions. A short reason
for retaining a structure is sufficient where a proposed split would add more
indirection than clarity; do not turn that decision into another approval stage.

A. Separate a numerical tensor field from its constitutive interpretation.
   Prefer a numerical component that owns a space and tensor coefficients, while
   material history owns the choice between physical tau and auxiliary T/R.
   The generic field should not recursively own another constitutive history.
   Keep common projection/advection reusable. A small material-side composed
   structure is preferable to a new hierarchy of history plugins. Preserve the
   serialized packet ordering and identities through the existing persistence
   boundary; internal type cleanup is not a checkpoint conversion.

B. Separate FE-space data from fault-dependent observation caches.
   TensorHistory::Space should clearly own its FE, DoFHandler, index sets, and
   appropriate numerical geometry data. The point cache containing phase values
   and NormalProfileProjection has a material/fault dependency and belongs with
   those observations. Keep purely numerical mass/projection caches where they
   are useful. Preserve the existing sharing, invalidation, clone, and threading
   semantics when moving caches; a new owner must not produce stale observations
   or repeatedly rebuild an expensive cache on every evaluation.

C. Give FEStrainState and its update meaningful internal groups.
   Separate constitutive state, profile/native FE data, derived observations,
   and attempt metadata where their lifetimes differ. Group related fields that
   are always interpreted together. Use composition within the existing owner
   where sufficient, rather than adding managers and duplicate state owners.
   In particular, displacement_history has different block meanings in native
   FE and DG configurations: document or expose those meanings clearly instead
   of blindly renaming the entire vector to phase or stress.

D. Make numerical and material projection responsibilities honest.
   Consider keeping phase/gradient sampling in a material-side adapter and
   passing the evaluated field to the numerical mass-projection operation.
   Reuse existing FE infrastructure. Avoid a general callback pipeline. If a
   routine intentionally remains phase-field-specific, locate/name/document it
   accordingly rather than advertising it as independent tensor infrastructure.

E. Join or split code by responsibility and use.
   Tensor storage and advection have distinct APIs; consider separate headers
   when that reduces coupling. Keep tightly related state definitions or small
   implementation helpers together where this reduces navigation. Split a long
   routine at an actual mathematical boundary only when the resulting interface
   makes its inputs and effects clearer. Do not create one class/file per step,
   replace short expressions with chains of wrappers, or move a monolithic
   class's methods between files and call that modularization.

Prioritize comprehensible ownership and a readable direct-tau path. Preserve
the separated reference/default configurations and distinct cohesive histories.
The direct path should not require reading the separated derivation to understand
its stress update. Do not use this task to select a new default or retire a
supported formulation.
