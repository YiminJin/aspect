1. Selected task and expected result

Implement, integrate, and verify a bulk-corotational stress update for the
particle-free mature frictional-fault model. The localized fault slip is to
be removed from BOTH the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of the velocity
gradient. This task explicitly selects a constitutive change. Earlier rules
to preserve total-material Jaumann spin do not prohibit this selected change.
Do not stop at another audit or a standalone utility: complete the native
history lifecycle, bounded coupled comparisons, restart checks, documentation,
and a clear recommendation for using the corrected model.

The selected interpretation is that the diffuse fault represents localized
relative slip, while the stress history represents the surrounding Maxwell
material. Pure tangential translation of two fault walls must not rotate that
bulk stress merely because the velocity jump is smeared over a finite width.
This is a kinematic/constitutive closure for the present small-elastic-strain,
objective Maxwell model, not a universal theorem for every shear-band model
or a replacement by finite-strain hyperelasticity.

Scope: mature particle-free DG stress, moving direct physical stress and the
retained separated representation, plus the stationary mature DG objective
anchor so selection does not silently fall back to total spin in that path.
Keep the existing native spatial projection and accepted-gradient Lie ordering.
Preserve the ordinary ASPECT timestep/solver ownership introduced by refactoring.
The particle-history and evolving cohesive paths need an explicit applicability
audit, but do not silently apply the mature formula to them. Their source rates
and kinematics differ; reject this new selector for unsupported combinations.

Retain the existing total-spin mode as an explicitly identified comparison.
Activate bulk spin in the new fixtures and maintained examples for this task.
Do not relabel old spin-enabled trajectories as corrected results. No server
submission, long physical campaign, or production-checkpoint conversion is
part of this task. Complete the native fix and bounded evidence locally.

2. Kinematic derivation and selected continuum law

Convention: L_ij = partial_j v_i, sym(A)=(A+A^T)/2,
skw(A)=(A-A^T)/2, D_t=partial_t+v.grad. All physical stresses are spatial
3-D deviatoric tensors represented under the existing plane-strain convention.

For a piecewise smooth velocity with jump [v]=V s across a fault with normal n,
the distributional gradient consists of the regular gradient plus

    [v] tensor n * delta_fault.

Regularize that jump term with the EXISTING localized fault-rate operator:

    J_f(x) = chi(x) V_tilde(x) [s_tilde(x) tensor n_tilde(x)],
    chi = h(phi)/I_tilde,
    s = shear_sense * tangent,
    S = sym(s tensor n).

Here J_f has units 1/s and is a full, nonsymmetric rank-two tensor. It is a
rate field, not the gradient of accumulated slip and not a stored history.
Define

    L_b = grad(v) - J_f,
    D_f = sym(J_f) = chi V_tilde S,
    W_f = skw(J_f) = chi V_tilde (s tensor n - n tensor s)/2,
    W_b = skw(L_b) = W - W_f.

Because s.n=0, J_f is trace-free, and the existing symmetric bulk forcing is
unchanged:

    dev3(sym(L_b)) = D_3(v) - D_f.

Use the following physical stress equation:

    D_t tau - W_b tau + tau W_b + (G/eta_b) tau
        = 2G [D_3(v) - D_f].

Call this a bulk-corotational or bulk-spin Jaumann-type rate. The ordinary
total-velocity Jaumann rate uses W; changing its spin changes the constitutive
law even though the numerical rotation algorithm is reused.

Do not subtract a viscous strain rate from W. A symmetric Maxwell relaxation
rate does not determine a further spin. Do not call W_b an exact finite-strain
elastic polar-rotation rate; that would require additional distortion state.

Objectivity check: for an observer rotation Q(t),

    L* = Q L Q^T + Omega,  Omega=dot(Q) Q^T,
    J_f* = Q J_f Q^T,
    W_b* = Q W_b Q^T + Omega.

Thus the selected bulk-corotational stress rate transforms objectively. The
observer angular velocity is retained; it is not removed with the slip spin.

Energy check, for the constant G already used by this model:

    E = tau:tau/(4G),
    D_t E + tau:tau/(2 eta_b) = tau:D_3(v) - tau:D_f.

The rotation term has zero contraction with tau. The correction introduces no
new dissipative term. This continuum identity does not eliminate DG projection
losses, transport errors, or time-splitting errors; account for them separately.

For curved faults, the singular jump contribution remains [v] tensor n locally.
Use the same local segment frame and support as the current symmetric source.
Do not remove all gradients caused by slip. Tangential changes in V, curvature,
and spatially varying extensions also generate regular gradients, which remain
in L_b. For v=H(rho) j(x), chi=H'(rho), n=grad(rho), j=V s, the independent
manufactured reference is

    grad(v) = j tensor [chi n] + H grad(j),
    L_b = H grad(j).

No global continuous 'bulk velocity' is required: L_b can be an incompatible
distortion rate. Do not construct v_b by subtracting a guessed slip velocity,
replace stress advection by v_b, differentiate the existing symmetric S to
invent a spin, or add curvature terms to J_f without changing the kinematics.
This closure preserves the current finite-width localization. It does not by
itself prove a general sharp-interface limit or repair existing tip errors.

3. Exact match to current source conventions

At the reviewed revision, localize_surface_values in
source/material_model/phase_field_fault/dg_objective.cc returns

    2 h(phi) a_tilde / I_tilde * S.

With a=V this is 2 D_f, NOT J_f and NOT D_f. With a=Z it is the auxiliary
stress correction B, with stress units. Do not use Z, Z/dt, or a difference
of Z as the instantaneous slip rate.

Refactor the existing evaluator enough to share one association/localization
sample between symmetric source and nonsymmetric slip-gradient evaluation.
Keep arithmetic/call semantics for the total-spin control as stable as possible.
The shared sample must carry the relevant fault/segment identity, active flag,
xi, signed local frame, h(phi), and normalization, with explicit state ownership.
It may return scalar weights/frame data or the tensor; avoid creating a second
geometry or normalization implementation.

For segment j and its current state's xi:

    V_tilde = (1-xi) V_j + xi V_(j+1),
    I_tilde = (1-xi) I_j + xi I_(j+1),
    q = h(phi) V_tilde / I_tilde,
    J_f = q outer_product(shear_sense*tangent, normal).

Interpolate V and I separately, then divide, exactly as existing mechanics does.
Do not interpolate V/I instead. Use the same actual association and activation
rules; there is no new endpoint-clamping closest-point map, continuation past
physical tips, normalization floor, phase threshold, or cap. Inactive source
means J_f=0. Respect existing unsupported multi-fault restrictions rather than
silently selecting/summing branches not admitted by mechanics.

The symmetric consistency test is

    2 sym(J_f) == existing localize_surface_values(V)

at matched points/states, to roundoff. Derive W_f from the full dyad. The
symmetric tensor alone does not uniquely determine its antisymmetric partner.

For the 2-D rotation helper define

    omega_b = 0.5 * [(L_10-J_f,10) - (L_01-J_f,01)],
    alpha_b = dt * omega_b,
    Q_b = [[cos(alpha_b), -sin(alpha_b)],
           [sin(alpha_b),  cos(alpha_b)]].

For tangent=(1,0), normal=(0,1), shear_sense=+1:
J_f,01=q and omega_b=(L_10-L_01+q)/2. Pure slip L_01=q gives zero.
This sign and factor of two must be independently tested.

4. Numerical update: freeze a coherent accepted bulk spin

Use the existing first-order accepted-state Lie discretization. At the beginning
of an attempt from accepted time t_n, before geometry/profile staging or mutable
Newton rates, freeze the following coherent state:

    v_h^n, committed V_f^n, phi_h^n, I_f^n,
    accepted fault geometry, shear sense, association support/version.

At each bulk rotation quadrature point x_q on the fixed mesh compute

    L_q^n = grad(v_h^n)(x_q),
    J_f,q^n = h(phi_h^n(x_q))/I_tilde^n(x_q)
              * V_tilde^n(x_q) * [s^n(x_q) tensor n^n(x_q)],
    omega_b,q^n = axial(skw(L_q^n-J_f,q^n)).

Retain these unprojected quadrature values as attempt-local data keyed by cell,
quadrature identity/order, and accepted-state identity. In 2-D one scalar per
point suffices. Compute before the live manager is swapped to candidate geometry.
Do not cache alpha independently of dt: a retry can change dt. Recompute or
validate/reuse omega from the same accepted parent and form alpha with retry dt.
If another evaluation path uses different points, explicitly evaluate against
an immutable accepted source context rather than reading candidate global data.

This snapshot is Eulerian omega_b^n(x), frozen over the interval, consistent with
the currently accepted-gradient Lie scheme. Do not additionally advect the spin
snapshot after advecting stress. A characteristic/midpoint treatment would be a
separate integrator change. Matching all subtraction inputs is essential even
though the whole method remains first-order accurate in time.

The specific trap is that rotate_material_history currently runs AFTER
stage_material_geometry and project_material_tensor reads the candidate profile
with the accepted velocity gradient. That is harmless for total spin, which did
not use phi, but is wrong for the new lagged subtraction. Merely inserting a call
to the current live localize_surface_values into that callback mixes states.

Then complete the existing native step:

    tau_adv = A_h(v_h^n, dt) tau_h^n,
    tau_bar = P_h[Q_b^n tau_adv (Q_b^n)^T],
    beta(x) = exp(-G dt/eta_b(x,t_(n+1))),
    kappa(x) = -eta_b(x,t_(n+1)) expm1(-G dt/eta_b(x,t_(n+1))),
    tau_h^(n+1) = P_h[ beta tau_bar
                       + 2 kappa (D_3(v_h^(n+1))-D_f^(n+1)) ].

A_h remains the current componentwise DG stress transport with full material
velocity. P_h remains the existing quadrature-defined DG mass projection.
Geometry/profile staging stays in its existing place. Endpoint D_f uses endpoint
geometry/profile/normalization and the current private slip-rate iterate, as it
already does. Freeze tau_bar throughout the coupled solve; outer iterations
repeat reaction, not transport or rotation. No new spin derivative belongs in
the current Newton Jacobian because both accepted v and accepted V are fixed.
Do not accidentally introduce W_b(v^(n+1),V^(n+1)) into this lagged scheme.

Keep native pointwise subtraction before the finite rotation; do not independently
smooth/project W_f, L, or omega to force cancellation. A discrepancy between native
FE grad(v_h) and nonpolynomial J_f is a spatial resolution error to measure.
Cancellation is exact in matched manufactured fields and convergent in resolved
FE fields, not guaranteed to roundoff for an arbitrary coarse fault profile.

6. Verification: one completed implementation and comparison package

Use independent references, rather than tests that call the same helper twice.
Integrate the following checks into the existing test/fixture infrastructure.
Use bounded local runs, normally at most four build workers. Extend existing
tests where they already cover a requirement, rather than duplicating a suite.

A. Source identity and orientation.
   Test 2 sym(J_f) against the existing mechanical source at matched states;
   arbitrary segment angles, both shear senses, varying V and I, inactive points,
   and accepted support near finite ends. Check ratio-of-interpolants explicitly.
   Compare with a componentwise independent dyad/rotation formula. In compatible
   algebraic cases expect errors near roundoff relative to the operands.

B. Pure localized slip with nonzero anisotropic initial stress.
   Prescribe grad(v)=J_f, first with representable constant data, then with a smooth
   resolved profile. Bulk spin and symmetric bulk forcing vanish. At fixed material
   points without transport effects, tau(t)=exp(-Gt/eta) tau(0); at eta=infinity
   it is constant. Use a representable uniform stress or account for transport
   independently. Repeat fixed accumulated slip over several widths. The old
   total-spin control must reproduce alpha=-delta/(2w); the new control must
   remove that systematic rotation. For FE profiles report h/width refinement
   and absolute residual spin, not an unexplained tolerance relative to huge
   cancelling total/fault terms. Do not require exact cancellation on unresolved
   nonpolynomial fields.

C. Slip plus genuine rotation and no-slip limits.
   Prescribe grad(v)=Omega+J_f. Recover W_b=Omega and the independent stress
   solution exp(-Gt/eta) Q(t) tau(0) Q(t)^T. Include arbitrary orientations/signs,
   the zero-total-spin/nonzero-fault-spin case, V=0, and inactive support.
   Check plane-strain norm/trace and continuum rotation work. No-slip should
   reduce to the retained total-spin results within the existing arithmetic
   contract. No-fault/default regressions remain unchanged.

D. Moving profile and time-level isolation.
   Construct a translating slip profile for which grad(v^n)=J_f^n at sampling
   points. Verify omega_b^n=0 before and after candidate geometry binding, and
   after perturbing private Newton rates. Rejection with a different retry dt
   must reproduce a fresh attempt from the same accepted state. A test that
   would pass even if endpoint phi/I/frame were used is insufficient.

E. Curved/varying-slip kinematics and observer covariance.
   Use independently differentiated v=H(rho) j(x) to verify the residual
   H grad(j), including its nonzero spin. This shows that genuine regular
   gradients have not been erased. Keep this analytical/local test separate
   from native curved-geometry capabilities not yet admitted by the model.
   Test the observer transformation of L, J_f and W_b; for the full Lie scheme
   report temporal convergence under time-dependent superposed rotation rather
   than claiming exact discrete covariance.

F. Bounded native mechanics and lifecycle.
   Run a prescribed-slip native fixture and short fully coupled mature RSF
   cases, including constant and layered viscosity, a translating fault, direct
   and separated histories, and a small time-refinement pair. Use nonzero
   stress so rotation is observable. Report tau components, shear/normal
   tractions, V, state, energy terms, total/fault/bulk spin, and nonlinear work.
   Include serial/MPI consistency, a same-law restart (preferably with changed
   MPI rank count), forced rejection/retry, and incompatible-law restart refusal.
   Confirm stationary DG uses the same law and exact-zero shortcut correctly.
   Rebuild matching checker DSOs after state-layout changes.

Diagnostics should report max/L2 total spin, fault spin, bulk spin and dt*bulk
spin, plus expected residual for manufactured cases. Where parent data should
be identical, check parent/geometry/profile/rate identity. Keep diagnostics
compact. Physical differences from total spin are expected and are not reasons
to tune friction, viscosity, normalization, filtering, or solver tolerances.

Existing tests of total-spin rigid rotation, simple shear and width sensitivity
remain useful legacy controls. A passing correction test establishes the chosen
kinematic property; it does not establish event accuracy or a sharp-tip limit.
If a known finite-end spatial error dominates a coupled run, measure it separately
and finish independent spin checks; do not disguise it with extra smoothing.
