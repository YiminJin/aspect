2. Construct an equilibrated layered background, with minimal benchmark code
===========================================================================
Use a smooth positive depth-dependent viscosity eta(y), constant G, and a
dipping mature fault that samples both sides of the transition. A viscosity
contrast of ten is a reasonable first qualification case. Choose the physical
transition width once and resolve it; do not shrink it with h. Choose duration
so lower-layer relaxation is appreciable while the upper layer retains memory.
Keep the present friction parameters and growth disabled to isolate this work.
This is an algorithmic layered benchmark, not yet the final crustal parameter set.

Do NOT insert eta(y) into the homogeneous constant-shear formula while keeping
the old affine velocities and claiming a consistent layered reference. For a
one-dimensional background v=(U+u(y,t),0), zero body force requires spatially
constant shear stress, and pressure must balance the varying normal stress.

An economical background construction follows directly from the existing split
law. Verify the conventions against the current code before implementing it:

  Given accepted background tau_n(y) and shear gradient gamma_n(y)=du_n/dy,
  rotate with the existing accepted-spin convention for the attempted dt:
      tau_bar(y) = Q_n(y) tau_n(y) Q_n(y)^T.
  Let beta(y)=exp(-G*dt/eta(y)) and
      kappa(y)=eta(y)*(1-exp(-G*dt/eta(y))).
  Prescribe a fixed horizontal velocity difference Delta_u from bottom to top.
  With b(y)=beta(y)*tau_bar_xy(y), the endpoint constant shear traction is
      q = [Delta_u + integral b(y)/kappa(y) dy]
                        / [integral 1/kappa(y) dy].
  Then gamma_next(y)=[q-b(y)]/kappa(y), and integrate gamma_next from the
  prescribed bottom velocity to obtain the background velocity profile.
  The normal components follow the same local rotation/relaxation law since
  the background normal strain rates vanish. Set
      p_next(y)=tau_yy_next(y)+p_ref,
  so top traction is (q,-p_ref).

This is a scalar quadrature calculation for benchmark boundary data, not a new
production mechanical solver. No fault contribution belongs in this exterior
reference: J_f=0 there, so bulk spin equals total spin. The prior layered
background construction therefore remains applicable after the correction.
Reuse and finish its interrupted implementation rather than replacing it.
With constant eta it must reduce to the existing homogeneous split
background. Check the integrated velocity difference, constant shear traction,
normal-force balance, and reference quadrature accuracy before the campaign.
Do not replace the actual coupled ASPECT velocity with this prescribed field
inside the physical fault runs.

During pure transport, supply tau_n(y) at incoming faces: background motion is
horizontal, so its unreacted parent field is unchanged during that substep.
Supply the endpoint background velocity and traction to the mechanical boundary
plugins. Initial mechanics must retain the actual .1-s initialization convention
and zero accepted stored stress; its velocity profile need not be affine when
eta varies. Do not count initialization as a published physical stress step.
Pressure is not generally zero throughout the layered background even if p_ref=0.

Support the actual accepted timestep sequence rather than t/dt or an integer
step formula. A rejected attempt must not advance background history. Keep
candidate evaluation separate from accepted publication, and restore the same
background on restart through existing plugin/lifecycle facilities. Keep these
reference-specific details in the benchmark plugin. Reuse the existing physical
stress callback; do not introduce a generic single-step driver, new execution
framework or additional production history representation.
