1. IMPLEMENT THE ELEMENT BY REUSING THE EXISTING DG MACHINERY

Name the class FE_DGQBernstein. Prefer deriving from dealii::FE_DGQ<dim,spacedim>,
using its protected constructor that accepts one-dimensional polynomials, as
FE_DGQLegendre and FE_DGQHermite do. FE_DGQ already derives from FE_Poly and owns
useful DG topology, interpolation/transfer machinery and polynomial evaluation.
Derive directly from FE_Poly only if inspection reveals a concrete incompatibility
that makes the additional implementation necessary; explain that decision.

Inspect the installed deal.II sources, not just the online API. Relevant upstream
references are:
https://dealii.org/9.7.0/doxygen/deal.II/fe__dgq_8h_source.html
https://dealii.org/9.7.0/doxygen/deal.II/fe__dgq_8cc_source.html
https://dealii.org/9.7.0/doxygen/deal.II/polynomials__bernstein_8h_source.html

The library supplies generate_complete_bernstein_basis<double>(degree). Start
from its existing facilities rather than copying FE_Bernstein or FE_DGQ. The
continuous FE_Bernstein class is not the proposed element.

Implement or verify the operations actually required by ASPECT: correct L2
conformity and cell-local DoFs, tensor-product numbering, shape values and
derivatives, face support/orientation, clone(), name/registration, interpolation
and polynomial transfer. Override inherited nodal assumptions where necessary.
Keep the element independent of fracture, RSF, phase bounds and history ownership.
Use a normal project-owned location/namespace consistent with existing extension
classes; no required patch to the user's installed deal.II library.

The Bernstein constant mode has every coefficient equal to one. Do not copy
FE_DGQLegendre's first-coefficient-only constant mode. Degree two is the supported
phase target; do not enlarge the physics scope to arbitrary-order phase solves.
If the element accepts other degrees, give that interface honest behavior and
small polynomial-level coverage rather than silently hardcoding Q2 everywhere.

Bernstein coefficients are not nodal point values. Do not populate ordinary
unit_support_points with a fictitious cardinal interpretation. Generalized
support points may sample a function, provided the corresponding conversion
computes Bernstein coefficients. For Q2, use the same native interpolant as the
reference: b=E^{-1}a, where a contains its nodal values. Setting b_i=f(x_i) would
produce a different approximation and alter the seed.

Check numerical evaluation through the actual FEValues/FEFaceValues machinery.
The inspected deal.II Bernstein generator represents polynomials by power
coefficients. Mathematical positivity alone does not prove stable floating-point
evaluation near endpoints. Compare against product-form/de Casteljau evaluation,
including tiny positive phase and points near zero/one. Preserve the current
stable intact-endpoint behavior. If necessary, use existing stable polynomial
facilities or one localized evaluator fix; do not clamp evaluated phase or scatter
independent Bernstein formulas through material and solver code.

3. PRESERVE THE NUMERICAL METHOD AND ITS TWO DISTINCT BOUND CONTRACTS

With a=E b denoting the same polynomial in nodal and Bernstein coordinates,
the following identities provide the comparison contract:

  M_B = E^T M_N E,
  r_B(b) = E^T r_N(E b),
  J_B(b) = E^T J_N(E b) E.

For coupled residuals apply the transformation only to phase rows/columns;
mechanical coordinates and the stated row scalings are unchanged. The existing
bulk-tip solver already uses Bernstein coordinates, so its retained residual
and exact tangent should agree directly after consistent ordering.

For a linear semidiscrete transport generator, L_B=E^{-1} L_N E. Preserve the
same weak form, upwind flux, physical measures, timestep/CFL rule and quadrature.
With nonlinear limiting, also compare the resulting physical polynomial and
cell mean after each relevant operation; the linear matrix identity alone is
not sufficient.

Do not introduce mass lumping or reduce quadrature as part of this task. The
current transport assembles a dense local mass matrix with QGaussLobatto(4)
for Q2, rather than the three-point collocated diagonal mass approximation.
Changing basis does not require changing its integration. Preserve the existing
fracture Gauss5 and mechanical Gauss6 rules in the tip controls.

Growth bounds:
  Keep b_parent <= b <= 1 and the existing release-capable KKT solve. This
  coefficient box is sufficient for polynomial bounds/irreversibility and is
  more restrictive than all pointwise-admissible Q2 fields. Native Bernstein
  storage does not justify changing that admissible set. Preserve zero-clock
  seed equilibration and exact lower-bound arithmetic.

Transport bounds:
  Preserve the existing subdivision-based polynomial enclosure and the
  mean-preserving scaling limiter. A bounded polynomial can have Bernstein
  coefficients outside [0,1]; do not replace the enclosure by coefficient
  clipping or unnecessarily restrict every transport polynomial to the growth
  coefficient box. Express the same scaling directly in Bernstein coordinates:
  b_i_new = mean + alpha*(b_i-mean).
  Use physical JxW-based means. The arithmetic mean of coefficients applies on
  affine cells, not generally on mapped cells. An inadmissible mean remains
  an update failure; it is not clipped.

Irreversibility follows the existing material-history/transport ordering. Do
not compare old and new coefficients at the same spatial cell as a substitute
for a transported material obstacle. Do not activate moving growth in the
fixed-reference tip control or alter the mature growth-off configuration.

Retain direct tau/A histories, full plane-strain tensor conventions, the same
section integration/work adjoint, trial-parent immutability, rejection and one
joint publication. Keep mechanics 1e-9, phase KKT 1e-7 and the existing independent
ledger/dissipation gates for the comparison cases. No physical parameter tuning.

4. INTERPOLATION, TRANSFER AND RESTART MUST KEEP THEIR MEANING

Provide polynomial-exact interpolation between nodal and Bernstein Q2 and exact
refinement embedding. Reuse inherited deal.II machinery where it satisfies the
contract and verify it with independent polynomial tests. Child Bernstein
coefficients can be obtained by subdivision; constants and the represented
physical field must be preserved.

For coarsening, distinguish polynomial projection accuracy from physical bounds
and irreversibility. Check the element's restriction on a polynomial that is
exactly representable on the parent, and conservation for the supported mapping.
Do not claim that arbitrary child fields can be coarsened without information
loss or that ordinary L2 projection preserves the phase obstacle. Maintain the
current physical AMR restrictions; this task does not require a new general
adaptive irreversible-phase algorithm.

Record the actual basis in checkpoint/FE metadata. Old nodal arrays must never
be silently interpreted as Bernstein coefficients, even if vector sizes match.
Preserve old checkpoints. Distinguish constitutive-law identity from numerical
basis identity and implement either an explicit compatible conversion or a
clear rejection for incompatible representations.

For the bounded old/new active-state comparison, a narrow benchmark import

required accepted phase/obstacle copy, while preserving physical tau/A,
