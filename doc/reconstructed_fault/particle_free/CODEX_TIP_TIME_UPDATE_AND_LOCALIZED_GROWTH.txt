1. DERIVE AND IMPLEMENT ONE COUPLED MIDPOINT CANDIDATE

Keep the same continuous model, prescribed compression, signed section closure,
fixed direction/reference mesh, physical tau/A histories and single SIPG fracture
functional. Change time discretization explicitly; do not change h(phi), Gc,
RSF parameters, geometry, physical damping or the phase admissible cone.
Retain BE as the comparison mode. Add at most one focused time-integrator
selection for the integrated tip route; do not introduce an integrator framework.

Use the following candidate, verifying its derivation before wiring the coupled
solver. Superscripts n and 1 denote accepted beginning and new endpoint. Let

  dt = physical step duration,  u = dt*v_m,
  tau_m = (tau_1 + tau_n)/2,  A_m = (A_1 + A_n)/2,
  b_m = (b_1 + b_n)/2,        phi_m = sum_i b_m,i B_i.

The subscript m denotes the time stage. The coupled equations are

  (tau_1-tau_n)/(2G dt) + tau_m/(2 eta) = D3(u)/dt - w_m S,
  (A_1-A_n)/dt + (G/eta) A_m = w_m S,
  Q_m = 2G A_m/h(phi_m),
  Y_m = G h'(phi_m) (A_m:A_m)/h(phi_m)^2,
  (tau_m-Q_m):S = f_m,
  V_m,s = sum_{q in s} dn_q w_m,q.

As in the present model, singular expressions above denote their analytic
intact/fully-damaged limits. Never evaluate infinity times zero or regularize
them with an arbitrary phase floor. Retain the full 3-D plane-strain metric.

Assemble mechanics using stage stress and pressure, and the phase VI using

  R_b = [H_f b_m + ell_f - integral B Y_m]/K_f,
  b_n <= b_1 <= 1,

with the existing lower/free/upper signs. Here H_f and ell_f come from the
same authoritative quadratic AT1/SIPG energy. The obstacle applies to the
endpoint b_1. Do not solve a midpoint obstacle and then extrapolate beyond one
or clip the endpoint. With stage coordinates, the equivalent upper bound is
(1+b_n)/2; document the chosen coordinates and all factors of two.

A useful exact algebraic reuse is available: the existing BE point elimination
evaluated with duration dt/2, strain increment D3(u)/2 and phase phi_m supplies
tau_m, A_m, Q_m, w_m and Y_m for these equations. Recover tau_1=2*tau_m-tau_n
and A_1=2*A_m-A_n. This is a local algebraic identity, not an accepted half step.
Check it against the equations above independently. Reuse the stable rational
elimination, rather than duplicate a second constitutive law. Endpoint storage
must be evaluated from tau_1, A_1 and phi_1, not from the stage response.

For state evolution use the exact solution of the aging equation at a constant
stage rate, at both stage and endpoint. With k=abs(V_m)/Dc,

  theta(s) = theta_n*exp(-k*s) + [1-exp(-k*s)]/k,
  theta_m = theta(dt/2),  theta_1 = theta(dt).

Use the continuous k=0 limit theta_n+s and stable expm1-based arithmetic.
Evaluate signed regularized RSF at (V_m,theta_m), including radiation at V_m.
This advances the same aging ODE and preserves positive age; it is not exact
for an arbitrary varying physical rate. Accumulate signed slip with dt*V_m,
and distinguish any existing absolute-slip diagnostic.
Where necessary, separate the existing signed RSF evaluation from its age-update
choice so BE and midpoint share the friction algebra and root machinery. Keep
this refactoring local; do not duplicate an entire friction implementation.

Differentiate the complete stage law, including theta_m(V_m), stage-phase
dependence, local elimination and section condensation. The old BE monotonicity
bound does not automatically apply. Derive a sufficient condition for the new
law or an otherwise justified unique-root admission on the tested cases.
Retain the current scope excluding unqualified multiple-root selection; do not
increase radiation damping or alter a/b to make a difficult case pass.
The intended later instability campaign remains a separate qualification.

Use boundary data and reaction work at the same stage. The present controls
have time-independent prescribed velocities. For time-dependent data, document
the approximation to the imposed displacement increment. Keep u/dt and pressure
explicitly identified as stage quantities, not endpoint observations.

Quadratic stress energy satisfies its midpoint chain rule exactly. So does the

  Delta E_f = (H_f b_m + ell_f) dot (b_1-b_n).
