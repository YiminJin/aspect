The follow-up implemented the proposed true-residual restart and replayed the same loading diagnostic. [Subsequent test](STAGE4_TRUE_RESIDUAL_RESTART.md).

**The same-timestep recovery succeeded.** The steady-load pilot accepted step 79 at 6.32 ms and step 80 at **6.40 ms** on the original physical gates. Weak connected damage entered initially intact material at φ = 0.001. The stronger φ = 0.01 contour stayed within initial support, and neither interval formed a section. The limitation on the steady branch is the slow approach to favorable tip driving relative to the cost per interval. On the separate fourfold-rate diagnostic the first fixed-plane linear search exhausted its directions before evaluating a nonlinear trial. The effect of that load change on physical growth is therefore unknown. **Next task:** restart the shifted mechanics FGMRES once from its explicitly recomputed residual when its estimate passes but its returned correction fails. ## Matched boundary-rate diagnostic

The baseline near-tip mechanical driving increased by 0.1161 J/m³ per ms between 6.20 and 6.36 ms. The fracture derivative rose by 0.08514 over the same unit of time. At the second new midpoint, the physical time since the common parent is 0.12 ms. Raising the affine boundary velocity by a factor of four for the two intervals supplies an extra 0.36 ms of reference displacement at that midpoint. If mechanical driving initially scales with accumulated boundary displacement, its additional 0.0418 J/m³ is comparable to the measured near deficit. The farther probe would gain only 0.0316 J/m³ against its 0.1847 deficit. This is a scale estimate. The coupled branch measures how much the changing fracture derivative and RSF response absorb.

The diagnostic starts from the same native step-78 generation. Its displacement is continuous at 6.24 ms and its prescribed velocity on each of the four sides is piecewise constant in time. Both components multiply by four at that instant, retaining the affine divergence-free spatial mode. There is no displacement impulse or reset of stress, phase, slip, age or pressure reference. The solver imposes the new interval displacement and evaluates external work from the raw boundary reaction and that displacement with its usual midpoint factor. The loading change is confined to the benchmark input. The 6.40 ms steady-load checkpoint remains a separate trajectory.

The rate diagnostic exited with code 1 at 04:20 Pacific on November 19. Its exact masked solve exhausted 600 iterations with explicit residual 2.401e-4. For shifts 1 through 1e5 the mechanics FGMRES returned after 31 iterations each. Every estimate passed, while every explicit residual (3.52e-14 to 4.23e-14) exceeded its own tolerance (2.376e-14 to 2.381e-14). For shifts 1e6, 1e7 and 1e8 the phase obstacle exhausted 100 passes; its defects grew from 2.43e-14 to 3.01e-12. All 322 inner CG returns passed their explicit checks. The rejected trial reached no nonlinear line search, virgin-plane update, work observation or publication. The only restart in that output is the byte-identical step-78 parent. A later request to stop this trial found that it had already exited and sent no signal. [Rate diagnostic, input and parent hashes](../../../benchmarks/reconstructed_fault/particle_free/evidence/stage4_propagation_pilot/overnight-recovery/rate-probe/summary.json) · [Native log](../../../benchmarks/reconstructed_fault/particle_free/evidence/stage4_propagation_pilot/overnight-recovery/rate-probe/native.log.gz).

The proposed single true-residual restart was not tested within the overnight window. A future repair should use the remaining original Krylov budget and independently certify the recombined correction before any line trial. Every shifted mechanics return on the two new steady intervals already passed its first explicit check. The probe gives a reproducible contrary case on the same checkpoint. No temporal refinement, new direction-law experiment or further physical trajectory was started.
