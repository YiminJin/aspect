The fine screening fixture has a 2 × 1 m box, a seed tip at (1.5, 0.5) m, phase length 0.25 m, cell size 0.0625 m, six Gauss points per axis and a 5 ms midpoint interval. The full-section candidate resistance was recomputed at every sampled angle from the same physical stage predictor. The independent score sums squared pointwise tractions. At the most strongly driven virgin station, ξ = 1.58629 m, all three radii selected −8.5° on a 0.5° scan. Absolute scores depend on the measure and station width.

**Registration and center tracking.** The initial seed owns its incoming plane on contiguous exact-positive material support from the interior anchor to the declared tip. At either end, subsequent registration requires an eligible section. A preliminary two-ended screen assigned newly activated rear sections the incoming plane. The final rule rejects off-disk activation of a virgin section at either end, and its archive signature distinguishes the corrected lifecycle. The original two-ended test exercised this rejection at its opposite end. The qualified single-front specimen extends the seed to the upstream boundary, retaining the same forward taper, uniform material and mixed shear/normal loading. It is a separate physical fixture. Positive phase alone outside the disk cannot choose its plane. In a quiescent native specimen a detached phase tail of order 10⁻¹⁵ persisted through acceptance. Its sections stayed virgin and aged to 10.005 s. With the same tail and the physical shear load, nonzero fault history developed there. The candidate rejected before publication with a detached-formation diagnostic. This exposes the conflict between subsequent reorientation and storing existing Mode-II tensor memory. A phase cutoff, clipping that memory, or resetting age would change the model.

In a manufactured check of the actual accepted-center calculation, a connected 0.1 DG band advanced the center to the last supported graph station. Inserting a gap with phase 10⁻¹⁶ left the center at the seed despite a more distant 0.1 patch. The short coupled trajectories themselves kept their center at the seed; the manufactured check supplies the moving-center evidence. The 0.05 tracking level and R = 2ℓ are explicit choices for this limited-turn front. In the actual quiescent and loaded tail tests the detached seed input was identical; both used the final registration rule.

The default nonaligned discretization assigns one RSF state per complete projected strip. Each cell supplies its own chord integration breakpoints. The signed reduction V = ΣJw/Δs and the uniform force return use the same material-area weights. On the unchanged 8 × 4 Cartesian mesh at 30°, nine strips and 4,968 points covered 2 m². An independent intersection with the four specimen half-spaces, integrated over each strip width, gave maximum errors of 2.22 × 10⁻¹⁵ m in average transverse length and 2.45 × 10⁻¹⁵ for the polynomial integral. ## Accepted-step timing and choice of rule

The two ranks were bound to distinct cores with one numerical thread each. Timing covers preparation from the parent through candidate construction and restoration of the ordinary solver. The direct clock includes neighborhood construction, moment and angle work, candidate RSF solves, associated communication and trial-plane updates. Kernel weights are reused across angles. Full-section moments skip ineligible cells, and zero free rate uses the exact zero resistance. The effective difference includes the changed path through the coupled solve. Compilation, checkpoints, postprocessing and plotting are outside both clocks. The replay checker reads the final prospective angles and checks the parent station identity, ages, slips and formed planes. Replays are single diagnostic intervals.

The first horizontal and oblique steps performed 8,112 and 1,690 evaluations, including 2,028 and 1,014 nonzero-rate candidate RSF solves. Six forward horizontal sections registered. At later steps virgin sections still had nonzero driving and changed direction despite zero candidate free rates. Second and later endpoints agreed at output precision in physical phase, stress, shear, section rates, ages, slip, directions and formed flags. First-step differences peaked at 7.66 × 10⁻⁹ Pa in stress. All seven prescribed-angle replays used the same MPI configuration and search parent.

The median effective difference was −0.94%. The 66.9% negative difference on oblique step 3 occurred with **1,202 Stokes solves on both sides**, four tangent assemblies on both sides and identical endpoints. Ordinary Stokes solution time rose from 2.56 to 4.01 s in the replay. Reversing execution order gave 6.37 versus 11.07 s overall. Both runs again recorded 14,809 inner velocity iterations and 44,427 pressure iterations. The source of the per-iteration wall-time difference remains unexplained. This timing does not establish a physical or algorithmic advantage for searching. In the preliminary two-ended screen, individual wall-time differences after reusing the converged trial residual ranged from −8.64% to +6.10%; before that optimization the largest positive horizontal difference had been 11.8%. Direct and effective times overlap.

**Retries and timing noise.** At horizontal step 2, discarding two converged trials and then accepting the third gave 3.254 s for search versus 1.883 s prescribed, a raw **+42.1%** overhead. Both compared the same deterministic endpoint. Repeating in reverse order with identical front-check and rejection diagnostics gave 2.190 s versus 2.173 s, or **+0.79%**. In the first pair the individual search attempts took 1.32, 1.30 and 0.64 s for the same work. The second pair had consistent attempt times. The direct total for that repeat was 7.40 ms (0.34%). A third identical-diagnostics pair gave 1.964 versus 1.758 s (+10.48%). The median across the three forced-rejection pairs is +10.48%, close to the target; the worst raw observation is +42.1%. Under the two equivalent-diagnostics repeats the median is +5.64%. The timing conclusion rests on matched physical endpoints, ordinary loaded steps and repeated measurements; an individual laptop wall-clock measurement is too noisy to guarantee a 10% ceiling.

**Closed-form comparator.** Tensile stress is positive. Relative to the axis θ₁ of largest principal stress, the two Coulomb tangent directions are θ₁ ± (π/4 + atan(μ₀)/2), modulo π. The code evaluates the equivalent signed deviatoric moments in the reference frame using the localized preceding accepted stress. It takes the admissible conjugate closest to the incoming signed plane, keeps positive shear on a tie, and retains the incoming direction at degeneracy. Here μ₀ = 0.6 and mechanical RSF still uses 200 Pa prescribed compression. The zero-stress initial parent supplies no Coulomb preference. On single-front steps 2 and 3 direct Coulomb times were 0.30 and 0.28 ms, with matched-replay overheads of +2.25% and +2.70%. The resulting endpoints match their own prescribed replays. This is an alternative constitutive hypothesis; the data do not justify substituting it for integrated onset driving.
