1. MATHEMATICAL REVIEW AND IMPLEMENTATION CONTRACT

1.1 Variables and assumptions

tau = true physical deviatoric stress, Pa, full 3-D plane-strain metric.
A = physical recoverable cohesive strain tensor, dimensionless.
Q = cohesive conjugate stress, Pa; derived from A and phi except at a constraint.
D_f = bulk fault-deformation rate, 1/s.
D_3 = deviatoric symmetric velocity gradient.
lambda = G/eta, 1/s, with constant G>0 and matched relaxation times.
h(phi)>=0 = additional dimensionless compliance.
phi is the authoritative bulk damage field; theta>0 is RSF age.

Do not confuse A with total accumulated strain, interface damage a, or trial
stress. Using true tau removes auxiliary stress reconstruction, not the
recoverable cohesive state required to store energy. A scalar is sufficient
only for a fixed shear direction; retain the physical tensor for changing
directions. Reorienting an old scalar is not tensor-history transport.

The first coupled finite-tip implementation/qualification is a fixed-reference,
small-strain specimen with one explicit closed Mode-II direction and no material
advection. This is a controlled first qualification, not permission to bake
Cartesian normals, a serial-only solver or a permanent fixed-direction
assumption into general infrastructure. General material motion/free turning
must remain explicitly unsupported until their missing closures are supplied.
Do not describe this as a finite-strain model.

1.2 Verified local energy and rates

For h>0:
  psi_el = tau:tau/(4G) + G A:A/h,
  Q = 2G A/h,
  Y = -partial_phi psi_el |_(tau,A)
    = G h'(phi) A:A/h^2 = h'(phi) Q:Q/(4G).

Use:
  tau^circle/(2G) + tau/(2eta) = D_3 - D_f,
  A^circle + lambda A = D_f.

These imply the identity:
  D_t psi_el =
      tau:D_3
    - tau:tau/(2eta)
    - h Q:Q/(2eta)
    - (tau-Q):D_f
    - Y D_t phi.

The two viscous losses are distinct and nonnegative. The flow/friction law
must specify the last mechanical loss (tau-Q):D_f and make the appropriate
local or explicitly nonlocal dissipation nonnegative. This inequality alone
does not determine that law.

The full tensor algebra, compliance derivatives, phase force and endpoint
identity were checked symbolically for this handoff. Spin power vanishes for
isotropic quadratic energies because Q is proportional to A. This does NOT
select the physical spin or prove that any spin gives the same trajectory.
Specify the objective rates of tau and A from the selected kinematics. If using
W_b=skew(grad v-J_f), define J_f throughout the process zone first; D_f alone
does not determine its antisymmetric part. Do not extend the mature selector
to cohesive growth by merely removing an admission guard.

For D_f=w S and A=e S, S:S=1/2:
  w [1/s], e dimensionless, q=Q:S=G e/h,
  psi_coh=G e^2/(2h), Y=h' q^2/(2G).
Keep these factors of two consistent with the tensor implementation.

1.3 Intact and fully damaged limits

At h=0:
  A=0 is a constraint, Q is its mechanically selected reaction.
Use the mixed/one-sided limit, not an h floor or 0/0 evaluation. The scalar
relaxed force is Y(0)=h'(0) q^2/(2G), with q supplied by the chosen flow law.

The old constant-resistance return selects an excess-over-resistance reaction.
A smooth regularized RSF law has zero frictional traction at V=0 and generally
does NOT preserve that Coulomb sticking interval or onset threshold. Re-derive
the intact limit with the actual chosen kinematics and friction law; do not
copy the old excess formula after replacing its resistance by a rate function.

For the existing h=m phi(1+phi)/(1-phi)^2, examine phi=1 using inverse compliance:
  r=1/h=(1-phi)^2/[m phi(1+phi)],
  Q=2G r A, psi_coh=G r A:A, Y=-G r' A:A.
For finite A, r and r' tend to zero at phi=1, so these three quantities have
finite zero limits. r is singular at phi=0; use the intact constraint there.
No single naively evaluated formula is valid at both endpoints.
Check the coupled return and tangent limits, not just these energy limits.
Do not introduce a hidden phi cap to avoid the calculation.

A growth-disabled mature profile with phi<1 and cohesion manually absent is
not automatically this model's fully damaged limit. Identify the actual
reduction/calibration needed before claiming equality with the mature branch.

1.4 Phase law and one fracture inventory

Use only:
  E_frac = integral_Omega Gc/(c_alpha ell)
             [alpha(phi)+ell^2 |grad phi|^2] dx.

At fixed configuration:
  F_phi = Gc/(c_alpha ell)[alpha'(phi)-2ell^2 Laplacian(phi)] - Y.
Solve the variational inequality on phi_old <= phi_new <= 1.
For future material transport the lower bound is transported phi_old.
Use the inequality with both bounds; the simpler sign/complementarity form
applies away from the upper bound.

The derivative defining Y holds physical elastic states fixed. After local
elimination, the Newton tangent must include the induced changes of A, Q and
mechanics. Do not differentiate viscous/frictional losses as though they were
stored energy to construct an unjustified common minimization functional.
Pressure-dependent RSF can require a nonsymmetric coupled tangent.
KKT satisfaction alone does not prove uniqueness or global stability.

For the scalar homogeneous intact condition:
  h'(0) q^2/(2G) = Gc alpha'(0)/(c_alpha ell).
This is a diagnostic derived from the selected law, not an extra propagation
criterion. Use the full spatial phase equation at the tip. alpha'(0)=0 need

The energy is volumetric. Do not add Gc integral(a ds), vertex insertion energy,

1.5 RSF closure must actually be closed

This is the main missing physics in the attachment. Define, before coupling:
  - D_f and its admissible directions, including virgin material;
  - the physical V [m/s] used by RSF and aging, in relation to bulk rate [1/s];
  - pressure/normal-traction convention, closed-contact scope, and theta's owner;
  - friction/radiation power and its work-conjugate bulk/surface coupling;
  - mature-interior reduction and behavior where no reconstructed surface exists.

Reuse the selected regularized signed RSF law and aging equation where applicable:
  r_f = p_N A_RSF asinh[
          V/(2 V0) exp((mu0+B_RSF ln(theta V0/Dc))/A_RSF)]
        + eta_rad V,
  D_t theta = 1 - |V| theta/Dc.
Then r_f V >= 0 for p_N>0 and eta_rad>=0.
A positive endpoint aging update is
  theta+ = (theta- + dt)/(1 + dt |V+|/Dc).
This is a time discretization, not an exact update for arbitrary V(t).

A bulk rate cannot be inserted directly as V. Neither V=cell_size*w nor an
undocumented width proportional to ell is an acceptable conversion. A local

derives it; energy admissibility alone does not determine b.

1.6 Material motion needs additional work, not just an advected obstacle

Let K=Gc/(c_alpha ell), constant here. For a material gradient:
  D_t grad phi = grad(D_t phi) - (grad v)^T grad phi.
Consequently:
  D_t[K alpha(phi)+K ell^2|grad phi|^2]
    = K alpha' D_t phi + 2K ell^2 grad phi . grad(D_t phi)
      - 2K ell^2 (grad phi outer grad phi):D.
A material-volume balance also includes psi_frac div(v); Eulerian balances
have transport/boundary flux. For incompressible flow the last displayed
deformation power generally remains.

Therefore a general advected growing model needs a consistent configurational/
microstress contribution or an explicitly prescribed-geometry work accounting.
The attachment's local elastic identity alone does not close this balance.
Derive the corresponding stress/work and boundary terms before enabling that
capability. Do not add a shape-work "correction" defined by the observed residual.
Keep the initial fixed-reference propagation scope separate from the preserved
moving mature test; neither proves general moving growth.
