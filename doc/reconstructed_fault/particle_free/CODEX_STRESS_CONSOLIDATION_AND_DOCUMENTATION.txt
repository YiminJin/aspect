PART 1: THREE CONNECTED CODE IMPROVEMENTS

1. Give localization and tensor samples their actual meanings.

evaluate_mature_dg_memory currently localizes supplied surface values. With Z it
produces a stress-like memory field; with slip rates it produces twice the
localized slip-rate tensor. Calling both operations "memory" obscures the direct
tau path. Rename the existing operation to express its shared localization role,
and name its arguments accordingly. Choose a concise name consistent with nearby
ASPECT code. Document the exact interpolation/localization expression, factor of
two, frame, normalization, and the units implied by the supplied surface values.

Update declarations, definitions, callers, tests, comments, and current guides.
Do not introduce a new wrapper for every call or change the interpolation,
geometry association, support, or normalization policy. Keep genuinely different
constitutive interpretations at their material call sites.

In project_mature_reaction and related direct/shared code, replace local names
such as T that actually mean either physical tau or auxiliary T with clear names
such as anchor_values. Keep T/R terminology where the separated law actually
uses those quantities. The direct update must read naturally as physical stress
plus strain-rate forcing minus localized slip-rate forcing.

2. Use one implementation of the common tensor mass-projection algebra.

TensorField::project in source/material_model/history/tensor_projection.cc and
project_mature_reaction in source/material_model/phase_field_fault/stress_reaction.cc
currently duplicate cell mass assembly, RHS accumulation, local inversion, and
coefficient publication. Consolidate that common numerical work. Material-side
code should provide evaluated fields and retain constitutive sampling/meaning.

Support the existing single-field and multiple-field uses without independently
rebuilding the same cell mass matrix for each separated tensor. A straightforward
shared kernel or a small extension of the existing projector is sufficient;
do not invent a projection framework or an additional chain of adapters.

Preserve quadrature, whole-product projection, component ordering, locally owned
cell selection, accumulation order where practical, ghost-vector construction,
and collective participation. Retain the pre/post projection energy diagnostics
and their existing observational meaning. Cell evaluators must not introduce
MPI collectives. Respect the current supported dimensions and discretizations;
this task does not enable new spaces or change quadrature.

Inspect nearby projection implementations for straightforward reuse, but do not
force distinct native-source roundoff calculations or formed-side spaces through
an interface that changes their mathematics. Explain any intentionally separate
projection algorithm. Keep the useful boundary between material field sampling
and numerical mass projection.

3. Make outer and inner mechanical iterations explicit.

solve_reconstructed_fault_stokes currently calls itself recursively to implement
the projection-consistency iteration, using material_reaction_solving stored in
the material model as a recursion guard. Replace this with explicit solver-owned
outer control calling a clearly identified inner mechanical solve. Reuse the
existing inner solver body; do not duplicate it.

Remove the material-owned recursion-control flag and its guard plumbing. Inspect
all uses first, including diagnostic labels and history preparation/publication.
Preserve any real behavior through appropriate explicit inputs or existing state;
do not simply delete dependent conditions or move the same hidden global flag to
another class. The material may retain its actual projected-response data, but
must not own the solver's recursion mode.

Preserve these concrete behaviors:
- Every reaction candidate starts from the same transported/rotated anchor.
- The projection correction is fixed during one inner mechanical solve and
  refreshed between outer iterations.
- Direct mode still uses the correction where currently required, including
  constant-viscosity cases; preserve separated-mode activation rules.
- Keep the current convergence measure, 24-iteration limit, and tolerance.
- Carry the converged private slip-rate iterate into the terminal solve.
- The final residual/KKT checks and candidate staging occur in their existing
  order; the ordinary timestep controller still decides acceptance and publishes
  the joint state once.
- Initialization, non-publishing calls, other solver callers, reuse options,
  rejected attempts, and exceptions retain their contracts.

This is a refactor inside the coupled solver. Do not restore a special mature
timestep driver, step executor, callback pipeline, or new solver framework.
Document the explicit call sequence and the lifetime of its temporary data.

PART 2: A DETAILED, LINKED GUIDE

sources through existing documentation tools if useful and straightforward, but

JavaScript application, renderer, or build-system dependency for this task.
The Markdown must remain useful directly in the repository without a build.

The landing page should explain the purpose, the supported configuration, the
small number of mathematical stages, and two reading routes: understand the
algorithm, or find code to modify. Keep it short enough to scan. Link to the
detail pages rather than reproducing them. Include a compact current revision
context and clear links to validation evidence and unresolved limitations.

Organize the detailed content approximately as follows. These are topic
boundaries, not a requirement to create a page for each function:

A. Model and variables
   Explain the physical equation, its assumptions, and the step to the endpoint
   update. Define tau, material velocity, slip rate, strain rate, spin, phase,
   localization, Maxwell coefficients, and the relevant surface state. Give
   units, location (bulk or fault), time level, and tensor/frame conventions.
   Explain full 3-D plane strain and the relation eta_ve=kappa. Distinguish the
   auxiliary localization bookkeeping removed from the direct formulation from
   the retained physical Jaumann rotation. Use minimal notation and connect it
   to manuscript notation without proposing a new constitutive derivation.

B. Discrete stress update
   Explain the FE spaces and which coefficients are actually stored. Derive the
   cell mass/RHS projection from the weak form and show where the shared kernel
   performs it. Explain why projecting the complete product matters. Describe
   upwind transport, SSPRK3 stages, substeps, inflow choices, and objective
   rotation in enough detail to follow their code. Identify accepted versus
   current velocities, coefficient sampling, quadrature, and the actual splitting
   order. Explain the distinction between an exact local rotation and the full
   discrete update, including projection and splitting errors.

C. Geometry, profile, and localization
   Explain how material labels/vertices move, how support widths/normals change,
   and how the Q1 profile and irreversibility obstacle are transported. Distinguish
   material motion from relative fault slip. Describe the actual bulk-to-fault
   association, surface interpolation, normalization, and extension used by the
   code. Do not replace the implemented geometry with an assumed nearest-point
   construction. Explain default versus optional profile/inflow handling and the
   current treatment/limitations of finite ends. Link mathematical operations
   directly to the geometry, profile, and localization routines.

D. Coupled mechanical solve
   Show how candidate physical stress enters the bulk and surface residuals,
   friction response, and the implemented linearization. Explain what the inner
   solve holds fixed, what the projection correction contains, and how the outer
   loop updates it. State explicitly which tangent is local and which coupling
   is resolved by the outer iteration; do not describe the whole procedure as an
   exact monolithic Newton solve without evidence. Explain why the projection
   correction can be needed even with constant viscosity, the stopping measure,
   and the final solve with the converged slip guess. Include the new actual
   function sequence and compact pseudocode.

E. State and lifecycle
   Trace one attempted step from accepted tau through transport, rotation,
   candidate stress, and acceptance. Provide a table of actual owners/members,
   readers/writers, lifetime, and whether each item is physical history, scratch,
   or a cache. Explain staged geometry binding, bulk/surface synchronization,
   initialization, rejection/retry, exceptions, restart, and cache invalidation.
   Explain when copying a state copies vectors versus shares data. Put MPI and
   threading details beside the operations they affect, without obscuring the
   physical narrative.

F. Configuration, code navigation, and retained alternatives
   Provide a complete small direct-stress example and a clear layered-viscosity
   variant using existing bounded fixtures. Give actual commands and explain the
   expected outputs. Ensure these examples are reproducible from maintained
   inputs and do not depend silently on a developer's old output directory.
   Distinguish an input fragment from a complete runnable file. Explicitly set
   Material stress representation = direct physical stress and explain the
   legacy DG trial stress parameter spelling.

   Supply an equation-to-code map and a short "where to change..." table for the
   material law, friction, transport, geometry, projection, and nonlinear solver.
   Explain which modules a direct-mode reader can initially skip. Keep the
   separated T/R/B comparison and native/cohesive/reference distinctions in a
   clearly optional section or additional reference page if needed. State actual
   defaults, support limits, and validation status; the direct guide must not
   imply that all retained modes have the same physical meaning.
