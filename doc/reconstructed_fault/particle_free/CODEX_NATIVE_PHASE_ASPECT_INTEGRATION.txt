3. REUSE ASSEMBLY WITHOUT CHANGING THE TESTED ALGORITHM
====================================================
The ordinary ASPECT advection cell assembler incorporates BE/BDF2 time terms;
solve_advection() normally limits its final solution. Therefore calling those
functions unchanged would not preserve the tested phase method.

Keep the native phase method:
  - Material advection, dphi/dt + v.grad(phi) = 0.
  - Its conservative weak form includes the phi div(v) source; numerical Stokes
    velocity is not pointwise divergence free.
  - SSPRK3 transport subcycling, with frozen accepted velocity and stage-time
    boundary evaluation, and BP limiting at the required stages.
  - Whole-cell Bernstein bounds and physical cell-mean-preserving scaling.
  - SIPG fracture energy, residual and tangent from the same functional.
  - The existing constrained equilibrium/growth law with its mechanical and
    normalization derivatives and final KKT/energy acceptance.

Use ASPECT's assembler ownership, scratch/copy conventions, distributed algebra
and established deal.II cell/face machinery. Separate reusable spatial terms
from time-discretization and thermal/material prefactors where necessary. Share
actual scalar kernels with existing assemblers where this is a clean, limited
change; do not label a copied parallel implementation as infrastructure reuse.
Avoid turning this into a universal advection-framework rewrite. Keep the small
explicit-stage operation needed by phase within the ordinary simulator dispatch.

There is no need for an artificial global Krylov solve for each explicit DG
stage: its cell mass inverses are appropriate. Reuse existing linear algebra and
solver/preconditioner facilities for the constrained growth search. The growth
equilibrium cannot be replaced by a temperature time-step solve. Do not insert a
physical-time mass term, apply a generic post-solve limiter to equilibrium, or
disable its nonlinear residual because generic DG advection does so.

For each attempted step, growth uses the transported phase as its lower obstacle
where transport and growth are supported, and the accepted old phase in the
stationary supported case. Growth may increase the phase integral; do not impose
a transport-style fixed cell mean on the growth solve. Keep Bernstein obstacle
constraints explicitly documented as sufficient whole-cell conditions, generally
stronger than pointwise constraints. Preserve accepted parents through retries.

4. REMOVE CARTESIAN GEOMETRY ASSUMPTIONS CONSISTENTLY
==================================================
Generalize the phase spatial operators to valid static mapped quadrilateral
meshes in 2-D, including rotated/sheared affine and distorted bilinear cells.
Use the simulator Mapping and standard FEValues/FEFaceValues/FESubfaceValues or
FEInterfaceValues, following ASPECT's existing face traversal where practical.
The deal.II 9.6 step-74 SIPG example is a useful reference, not a new driver.

In dg_sipg.cc and dg_transport.cc, replace physical-axis assumptions together:
  - f/2 as a physical axis and 1-axis as the tangent;
  - physical face normals represented by a signed coordinate component;
  - coordinate-interval overlap as the actual cell-face intersection;
  - hand-built physical face quadrature and interval-based face measures;
  - coordinate widths used for penalties, transport bounds or inverse mapping.

Obtain normals, gradients, quadrature points and JxW from the mapping-aware FE
objects. Match neighboring quadrature locations and orientation, including
coarse/fine interfaces. Assemble opposite face contributions consistently across
MPI partitions. Preserve both SIPG consistency terms, its jump penalty and the
appropriate boundary terms. Use geometry-aware normal scales/trace estimates
and coefficient traces in the penalty. Do not pretend general heterogeneous
coupled fracture coefficients are implemented if the supported law remains
homogeneous; preserve clear physical admission rules.

Generalize the BP timestep condition as part of this change. The old aligned
Lobatto condition and 6/h face-loss estimate use Cartesian geometry; swapping
normals does not establish a valid positivity bound on a mapped cell. Derive and
document a sufficient condition using physical volume/face weights or suitable
mapped trace bounds. Verify constant preservation, admissible means and material
balance with the chosen quadrature, including nonzero div(v). Affine polynomial
exactness statements must not be copied to non-affine mappings without checking.

Bernstein enclosure remains a reference-element polynomial property. Its scaling
must preserve the physical mean computed with the correct Jacobian weights. Do
not clip means, renormalize globally, replace the physical upper bound with an
epsilon cap, or conceal geometry errors through numerical diffusion.

Replace the native wrapper's unconditional zero-inflow lambda with explicit
boundary data through the field's established boundary infrastructure. Keep
zero inflow as the selected compact-profile fixture data. Correct inflow/outflow
selection and stage-time evaluation matter; do not prescribe outflow values.

Document a deterministic, MPI-independent one-sided trace convention for fault
observations on DG faces, and use it consistently in mechanics, normalization
and derivatives. Do not rely on an axis-aligned vertex ordering to define the
physical trace or average traces before nonlinear localization.

Follow active geometric dependencies beyond these two files. Normalization
clipping/search and some stress-history optimizations also assume Cartesian
cells. Replace geometry-only shortcuts or use an existing correct mapped path
where available; preserve the stress equations and time integration. Do not
claim full coupled general-mesh support based only on a scalar operator test.
If a remaining stress/localization restriction requires a substantive separate
redesign, name the exact path and limitation, retain its truthful guard, and
finish the phase integration. No new stress formulation is selected here.

Dynamic AMR transfer, ALE/moving bulk meshes, 3-D fault physics and every boundary
topology are not mandatory additions. Static nonmatching faces are in scope.
An advected fault on a fixed mesh is not ALE. Avoid new Cartesian assumptions
in any reusable dimension-templated code.
