# Midpoint update closeout and scientific reference

The frozen package now lives in the [sibling reference directory](../../../../aspect-pf-rsf-reference-20261121/README.md). The [storage closeout](../WORKTREE_CLEANUP.md) records the relocated restart check and the local availability of the comparison data.

**Freeze the implemented midpoint update for refactoring. Treat the 18-strip trajectory as a diagnostic of timestep sensitivity.** The phase equation equilibrates at the midpoint under an irreversible obstacle. Publication carries that increment a second time to the endpoint. On this specimen, a second solve therefore has a new direction of decreasing fracture energy even under constant loading. An independent fracture-only solve reproduces the native second phase increment to within 0.014% in spatial L². The scientific limitation is confirmed for this preparation. The audit found no duration, chain-factor, work-accounting or publication defect requiring a native code change.

## What one physical step computes

Let Δ be the pending physical duration, h = Δ/2, S the section shear tensor with S:S = 1/2, and D₃ the plane-strain deviator. Subscripts n, m, 1 denote accepted parent, stage and published endpoint. Only a, pₘ/G and bₘ enter the global nonlinear vector. The material-point and section solves are eliminated within each trial.

The physical constraint bₙ ≤ b₁ ≤ 1 gives bₙ ≤ bₘ ≤ (1+bₙ)/2. With the stage as the unknown, the phase tangent is [H−∂(∫B Yₘ)/∂bₘ]/Kf including the condensed section response. Both the fracture Hessian H and the mechanical phase columns act on the native stage basis with unit chain factor. Using endpoint coordinates instead would give δbₘ = δb₁/2 in **every** stage phase column. The derivative of the implemented publication is 2I. Increment positivity follows from the nonnegative Bernstein basis. The incoming physical field can be admissible with a negative individual coefficient. Its minimum coefficient here was −0.008669. Depth-four subdivision of the reconstructed polynomials enclosed all four fields within [0, 0.200202] to 7 × 10⁻¹⁷. [Basis and enclosure](../../../include/aspect/phase_field/dg_bounds.h) · [Box residual](../../../include/aspect/phase_field/box_constraints.h).

Restart restores the accepted material clock, then applies the pending-Δ override before constructing the coupling and its cached h, relaxation, boundary increments and obstacle. Subsequent simulator steps use the ordinary timestep manager and the input's maximum Δ. Coarse parent/stage/end times were 5/7.5/10 ms. The half steps used 5/6.25/7.5 and 7.5/8.75/10 ms. The boundary function sees simulator endpoint time and prescribes a **steady** affine velocity on all walls in these inputs. It has no timestep-number forcing. Stage-time accuracy for explicitly varying loads is outside this reference. Recomputed wall velocities agreed to 3.5 × 10⁻¹⁸ m/s, area-mean strain rates to 6.5 × 10⁻¹⁷ s⁻¹, section forces to 2.85 × 10⁻¹⁴ Pa, and endpoint ages to export precision. Trial evaluation reads the same accepted tensors, sections and obstacle. Only final acceptance publishes them. The localized control checks immutable trials, two rollbacks, deterministic retry, single publication and restart. ## Why the additional step changes the phase

The 5 ms parent came from a seed with zero-clock equilibration disabled. To check its *own* endpoint, assemble g = ∇F(b)−∫B Y₁ dA and test admissible increases from b. At positive phase use the published A,φ in −∂Ψ/∂φ. At exactly intact points A=0, use the analytic intact reaction on the stored plane with published τ and θ and the last resolved section V. This last choice is conditional because an endpoint slip-rate root was never solved. The conclusion is independent of that choice. All 183 downhill rows at 5 ms already have negative fracture gradient, and any physical Y is nonnegative. Their projected increment residual exceeds 10⁻⁷ on all 183. After either first update, 169 rows still admit downhill increments. This one-sided endpoint test diagnoses the available next increment. The parent itself passed its stage equilibrium with projected residual 6.20 × 10⁻⁹. The 183-row endpoint finding therefore does not reverse an acceptance decision. [Endpoint and independent SIPG calculation](../../../benchmarks/reconstructed_fault/particle_free/reference_sections/midpoint_closeout.py).

Both native first stages had the identical 947 lower, 205 free and zero upper rows. Their largest coefficient difference was 3.33 × 10⁻⁶. Along each published phase increment the fracture energy fell 1.31735 J/m to its midpoint minimum (fraction 0.4999995 or 0.5000000), then rose again. The corresponding mechanical phase work was only 5.5505 or 0.1349 μJ/m. The two endpoints essentially agree in phase even where the mechanical histories differ. At the point of the largest first increment, (0.75121, 0.12507) m, the endpoint phases agree within 1.2 × 10⁻⁸. The table follows the point of **largest second increment**, (0.75368, 0.12078) m, across all four saved states.

**Decisive control.** Reconstruct the DG polynomial in each cell from saved material points, independently assemble the 1,152-row AT1 problem on the Cartesian mesh with natural exterior, arithmetic normal-gradient averages, penalty 10 × 3² × 2Kfℓ²/h_cell and each interior face counted once. Set Y=0 in this isolated calculation. The four endpoint energies agree with the native totals within 10⁻¹² J/m. Freezing the mechanical drive at zero and solving the same stage VI gives first and second endpoints within 8.40 × 10⁻⁹ and 9.99 × 10⁻⁷ m in L² of the two native half-step endpoints. Its second increment is 0.00742635 m versus 0.00742678 m coupled. All four independent quadratic solves have projected residual below 2.2 × 10⁻¹³. Slip acceleration is real in the discrete histories (1.40 to 14.92 mm/s peak between halves). It is unnecessary to invoke a friction branch switch to explain the phase excursion. Neither trajectory resolves its physical timing.

An endpoint-equilibrated alternative minimizes F(b₁)−D·b₁ over bₙ ≤ b₁ ≤ 1 and publishes the minimizer directly. At constant D a minimizing endpoint stays a minimizer when the next lower bound is raised to that endpoint. On the frozen specimen with D=0 this gives a 0.00574450 m first increment and zero second increment. An independently enumerable two-row quadratic gives the same distinction. Under fixed load its midpoint endpoints are (0.2,0) then (0.2,0.05), while both endpoint solves give (0.1,0). This validates a conditional endpoint correction **if endpoint equilibrium is selected**. Coupling it to midpoint mechanics would require a corresponding driving/work discretization. The current algorithm explicitly selects stage equilibrium, so this control changes no native law, acceptance criterion or checkpoint.

## Physical work and its limitation

For this plane-strain specimen Ψ = G r(φ) A:A with m r = 1 + 1/φ − 4/(1+φ). With qₘ, e and Yₘ from the table, the local chain defect is qₘ Δe − ΔΨ − Yₘ Δφ. For a term 1/(φ+c), substitution of the arithmetic midpoint gives (G/m) Δφ·uv/(u+v)² times (A₁/v−Aₙ/u):(A₁/v−Aₙ/u), u=φₙ+c and v=φ₁+c. Combine c=0 and −4 times c=1, with the Aₙ=0 intact limit. Thus a smooth increment away from the singularity gives a cubic local defect and a quadratic fixed-window sum. On a smooth strain/phase chord the accumulated absolute defects at 8, 16, 32 and 64 steps were 2.328 × 10⁻⁶, 5.821 × 10⁻⁷, 1.455 × 10⁻⁷ and 3.638 × 10⁻⁸ J/m³. The native first phase increment scarcely shrank when Δ halved.

The independent physical defect is Δ times wall reaction power minus the change in all three stored energies (elastic τ:τ/(4G), cohesive Ψ and discrete fracture F), minus Δ times the bulk viscous, cohesive viscous and friction/radiation powers at the stage. It recovers each native physical defect within 2 × 10⁻¹⁴ J/m. The remainder accounts for 0.093253 and 6.689062 μJ/m in the two half steps. Residual work of the accepted mechanical and phase equations accounts for the small difference from the physical defect. In the second half the singular compliance term contributes +6.745830 μJ/m and the regular term −0.056768 μJ/m. Of the absolute point contributions, 62.1% come from 0.001 < Δφ ≤ 0.01, 63.7% from the positive-normal flank and zero from previously intact points. The second-half negative contributions sum to −1.11 × 10⁻¹⁹ J/m. No new section registers. The residual/fracture ratio falls from 71.35% to 45.10% because added fracture work grows.
