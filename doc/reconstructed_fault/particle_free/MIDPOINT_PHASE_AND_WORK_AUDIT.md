# Why the first midpoint phase increment barely changes when the timestep is halved

**The timestep override and the steady boundary loading are consistent in the tested 18-strip specimen.** From the same 5 ms parent, the coarse step and the first half-step solve a rate-independent phase inequality on exactly the same 205 free coefficients. Along each accepted phase increment, the fracture energy alone has its minimum almost exactly at the midpoint. The scheme finds a stage close to that minimum and extrapolates to the endpoint. A second half-step then has another admissible direction of decreasing fracture energy. This explains the large phase sensitivity even though the nearly equal first increments correspond to different elapsed times.

The large work remainder has a separate, measurable source. In the 7.5–10 ms interval, compliant material is both sliding faster and changing phase. Midpoint evaluation of the nonlinear cohesive energy derivative along that change leaves 6.689062 μJ/m in the discrete chain rule. Two independent reconstructions reproduce it. The audit found no stale duration, load mismatch, incorrect bound, or accounting error to repair in this configuration.

## The phase inequality and its actual active rows

For this AT1 fixture, write F(b) for the discrete fracture energy with affine gradient H b + l. The phase rows are (H bₘ + l − ∫ B Yₘ dA)/Kf, where B is a native DG Bernstein test function. The endpoint relation b₁ = 2bₘ − bₙ converts coefficientwise irreversibility and the upper bound into **bₙ ≤ bₘ ≤ (1+bₙ)/2**. The same box is used for the Newton trial, line-search feasibility and the final projected residual. Publication applies the affine endpoint relation once. The nonnegative partition-of-unity basis extends the coefficient bounds to the phase field throughout each cell. These are stage equilibrium equations with endpoint feasibility. [Box and sign convention](../../../include/aspect/phase_field/box_constraints.h) · [Trial and acceptance](../../../source/simulator/solver_schemes.cc) · [Stage and endpoint equations](tip_treatment/time_integration.md).

Only the two intervals sharing the original parent were replayed, once each, with the existing phase-row observer. Re-evaluating the native classification from the stage coefficient, residual and row mass gave **947 lower, 205 free, 0 upper** in both cases. All 1,152 cell/local row identities matched. Each lower row was exactly at its incoming value. The maximum difference between coarse and first-half stage coefficients was 3.33 × 10⁻⁶. The normalized projected residuals were 1.45 × 10⁻¹² and 6.97 × 10⁻⁹, respectively, below the existing 10⁻⁷ phase tolerance.

The gradient of the quadratic fracture functional at the parent can be recovered exactly as 2∇F(bₘ) − ∇F(b₁). At 5 ms, 183 rows admitted an increase that would reduce fracture energy even with the mechanical driving removed. Zero-clock seed equilibration was disabled in the actual input. Along the **observed** increment d = b₁ − bₙ, the directional derivative ∇F(bₙ)·d was −5.26939 J/m for both durations. F fell 1.31735 J/m from parent to stage, then rose again by publication. The minimum of F(bₙ+λd) occurred at λ = 0.49999947 for the coarse solve and 0.49999999 for the first half-step. Stage mechanical driving work along d was 5.55047 and 0.134865 μJ/m, respectively. These are one-dimensional measurements along the native solutions, not an assumption about the unconstrained global minimum.

At 7.5 ms, 169 coefficients still admitted a fracture-energy-decreasing increase from the new lower bound. Of the second half-step's phase volume increment, 88.3% lay on those rows. The first-half endpoint fracture gradient applied to the observed second increment was −4.26538 J/m. Thus a second stage can redistribute phase substantially without changing the first interval's contact set or violating irreversibility. The peak stage slip rate rose from 1.3995 to 14.9161 mm/s between the two halves. Assigning an onset time or a converged growth trajectory from these endpoints remains unjustified. [Coefficient and directional-derivative measurements](../../../build-particle-free/strip-width/midpoint-audit/comparison.json).

## Where the work remainder comes from

At each material point write S for the stored shear plane tensor, e = 2A:S and qₘ = τₘ:S − fₘ for the signed fault traction after section resistance. The cohesive stored energy is Ψ = G r(φ) A:A, with r = (1−φ)²/[mφ(1+φ)] and the native intact limit A = 0. From the accepted tensors and section force we independently reconstructed qₘ, Ψₙ, Ψ₁ and Yₘ = m(1+3φₘ)qₘ²/[2G(1−φₘ)³]. The remainder is the physical-area sum of **qₘ Δe − ΔΨ − Yₘ Δφ**. This explicitly separates the change in cohesive energy from stage traction and phase driving work. The quadratic AT1 fracture functional has an exact midpoint chain rule.

| 5–10 ms | 119.665136 | 110.154574 | 5.550475 | 3.960087 / 3.960087 |
| 5–7.5 ms | 3.037651 | 2.809533 | 0.134865 | 0.093253 / 0.093253 |
| 7.5–10 ms | 270.996320 | 249.400664 | 14.906594 | 6.689062 / 6.689062 |

All entries are μJ/m. Direct reconstruction agreed to within 3 × 10⁻²⁰ J/m. Independently summing boundary work, endpoint elastic and cohesive energies, the reported fracture-energy change, and stage physical dissipation reproduced the full physical-work residual to 5.4 × 10⁻¹⁵ J/m. The small additional phase and mechanical residual work in the first half-step accounts for the difference between its physical-work residual (0.093895 μJ/m) and its midpoint remainder (0.093253 μJ/m).

A second check identifies the nonlinear term. Since m r(φ) = 1 + 1/φ − 4/(1+φ), the constant term in r has zero midpoint error. For each denominator φ+c the endpoint-only remainder is proportional to Δφ times the squared change in A/(φ+c), weighted by (φₙ+c)(φ₁+c)/(φₙ+φ₁+2c)². The intact limit for c=0 is evaluated along the chord with Aₙ=0. In the second half-step the 1/φ term contributed **+6.745830 μJ/m** and the −4/(1+φ) term **−0.056768 μJ/m**. This pinpoints the sensitivity to concurrent strain and phase change in lightly damaged material. [Independent endpoint calculation](../../../benchmarks/reconstructed_fault/particle_free/reference_sections/midpoint_audit.py).

In that largest-remainder interval, 62.1% of the accumulated absolute contributions came from points with 0.001 < Δφ ≤ 0.01. A further 25.7% came from Δφ > 0.01. Previously intact points contributed zero in the exports. On the positive-normal flank lay 63.7% of the absolute remainder. Longitudinally, 44.6% lay behind the seed center, 36.0% in the old taper and 19.4% ahead of the declared tip. Examples are cell 109 near (1.424, 0.803) m, carrying 7.52% with mean incoming phase 0.01165 and mean increment 0.00666, and cell 114 near (1.538, 0.678) m, carrying 6.16% with mean incoming phase 0.00412 and mean increment 0.00293. The cell positions and means use absolute remainder as the weight. Negative contributions summed to only −1.11 × 10⁻¹⁹ J/m in that interval. ## Evidence and scope

Both observer replays used the retained localized-selection executable and checker from the timestep comparison, two bound local MPI ranks, and the same generation 02 parent. Point, reaction, section and plane exports and the corresponding ledger rows matched the original physical results byte for byte. The retained solver and checker source hashes were checked against the worktree. The two material source files that had since changed were checked against the archived tested patch; their differences are confined to strip-subdivision configuration. The second half-step was analyzed directly from its retained outputs. [Replay identities](../../../build-particle-free/strip-width/midpoint-audit/identities.json) · [Previous matched timestep comparison](TIP_DIRECTION_AND_STRIP_RESOLUTION.md#bounded-timestep-comparison-from-the-accepted-5-ms-state).

The dated implementation realizes its documented midpoint law for this steady-loading case. The limitation is the use of a stage equilibrium and endpoint extrapolation for a rate-independent phase field that starts with substantial admissible fracture relaxation. If endpoint phase equilibrium is required for interpreting propagation, the next design comparison should test an endpoint-constrained phase update on this same parent, with slip, irreversibility and physical work assessed together. That would be a change to the time discretization.
