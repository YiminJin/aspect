4. ADVECTION AND THE BP LIMITER
==============================
With growth disabled the equation is material transport:
    partial_t phi + v . grad(phi) = 0,
or equivalently
    partial_t phi + div(phi v) = phi div(v).
Implement upwind DGQ2 with the correct material balance, boundary fluxes and
constant-state preservation. Do not silently replace it with density transport.
The native Stokes velocity is not guaranteed pointwise divergence-free. Derive
the actual discrete balance, including any source and time-stage quadrature.

Use a well-founded bound-preserving scaling limiter that retains each cell's
quadrature-consistent mean:
    phi_limited = mean_K(phi) + alpha_K * (phi - mean_K(phi)),
    0 <= alpha_K <= 1.
Do not clip nodal values or cell means, renormalize global phi, smooth the
profile, or limit the derived localization chi instead of phi.

The underlying update must keep cell means admissible. A scaling limiter cannot
repair an inadmissible mean while preserving it. Establish the applicable CFL,
quadrature and divergence-source conditions. SSPRK3 with limiting at the required
stages is the preferred transport time integrator; use scalar infrastructure
from existing implementations where appropriate. If transport subcycling is
needed, keep the physical accepted step and accepted-velocity convention intact.
An alternative time discretization requires a concrete accuracy/stability/cost
justification, not convenience of copying a temperature BDF solve.

Distinguish global physical bounds from aggressive donor extrema bounds. Avoid
flattening resolved smooth peaks unnecessarily. Standard Q2 nodal bounds alone
do not bound the polynomial everywhere. State and implement where bounds are
guaranteed. Protect all phase evaluations used by volume mechanics, fluxes,
normalization and fault integration; if a whole-cell guarantee is chosen, explain
the construction and its accuracy implications. Preserve the actual constitutive
admissible range; do not invent an epsilon cap to hide singular evaluations.

5. DG PHASE ASSEMBLY AND THE CONSTRAINED SOLVE
============================================
Implement the native phase-gradient operator using symmetric interior penalty
DG (SIPG): cell gradient terms, both consistency terms, jump penalties, and
appropriate weak boundary terms. Retain the existing physical length scale,
fracture coefficients, degradation/geometric functions and mechanical driving
terms. Define penalty scaling with local size, degree and coefficient, and
verify coercivity for the corresponding positive linear model problem.

The phase equation is generally a nonlinear equilibrium/obstacle problem, not
necessarily a linear Helmholtz equation. Do not insert the temperature equation's
time-mass term, heat capacity or conductivity interpretation unless the selected
phase formulation actually contains the corresponding physical/pseudo-time term.
Preserve the project's selected recoverable-energy formulation; do not restore
particle H as the governing history merely to make the solver simpler.

Use the SAME SIPG discrete gradient energy in energy evaluation, residual,
tangent and acceptance checks. Include the face terms in the energy, not just
the matrix. Retain the localization/normalization variations required by the
native mechanical-phase coupling and its work adjoint. Update those variations
for the complete phase FE space; increasing phase degree is not permission to
freeze I or omit nonlocal derivatives.

For a material step, first transport the accepted profile to phi_adv. In a
growth-enabled phase solve the immutable lower obstacle is phi_adv, with the
existing physical upper bound. In a stationary case the obstacle is the accepted
profile. Enforce the obstacle through a consistent constrained nonlinear solve,
such as an appropriately generalized active-set/semismooth method. Specify the
discrete admissible set: Q1-style coefficient inequalities do not automatically
imply pointwise Q2 inequalities. Constraints at a chosen point set or in a
positive basis need a clear interpretation and corresponding residual/KKT tests.

Do not solve an unconstrained Helmholtz problem and then apply the ordinary BP
limiter as the final phase update. This can violate phi >= phi_adv and invalidate
the equilibrium residual and energy acceptance. The BP limiter belongs to the
transport step; any correction within growth must be integrated into the
constraint algorithm and followed by proper constrained convergence checks.
Phase growth may physically change the cell averages and total phase integral.
The advection conservation requirement must not be imposed on growth.

Complete the DG constrained solver in supported phase-growth paths. Also verify
the transport-then-constrained-solve composition using a manufactured phase
problem with a moving obstacle. This does not authorize inventing a missing
moving-cohesive constitutive law or removing unrelated maturity/growth guards.
Clearly distinguish phase-operator capability from qualification of the entire
coupled growing-fault model.
